What Are Football Betting Odds and How Do They Work?
The betting odds you find on OddsPortal represent the likelihood of a specific event occurring during a match, with those prices provided straight from our trusted betting partners. Those football odds can be presented in three formats:
Fractional (e.g., 5/1):
Common in the UK, these show the ratio of profit relative to the stake.
Decimal (e.g., 6.00):
Popular in Europe, these indicate the total payout, including the stake.
Moneyline (e.g., +500):
Favoured in the US, positive numbers show underdog odds, while negative numbers show favourites.
For example, if Manchester United were odds of 2/1 to win and you placed a single bet of £10, you’d win £20 profit and £30 in total if your prediction is correct at the end of the game.
Factors Influencing Football Odds
Several variables affect football betting odds, including:
Team Form:
A team that has been performing consistently well all season or is on a winning or unbeaten run will likely have their odds shortened over time..
Player Injuries:
The best player on a team being absent can lengthen a team’s odds.
Historical Data:
Head-to-head results can shape odds if one team for example has been unable to beat their opponent over quite a few meetings between the two.
For example, if Real Madrid are up against Barcelona, Real Madrid’s odds could drop significantly if one of their star players is unavailable.

Well-researched bets give you the best chance of being most profitable when betting on football, but there is no guarantee of any bet being more profitable than others. The higher the football betting odds are means the more profit you will make, but it also means the possibility is less likely.
